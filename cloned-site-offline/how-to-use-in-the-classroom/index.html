Using wheelofnames.com in the classroom

Teacher Michelle Emerson has recorded an excellent video sharing ideas for how to use Wheel of Names in the classroom. Michelle has no connection to wheelofnames.com, except as a user of the website. If you have any questions about the website, please click the Feedback option at the upper right of this page instead of reaching out to Michelle!
1. Virtual equity sticks

Equity sticks is a jar or a cup with popsicle sticks that have every student's name on them. You would use the sticks to select which student should answer a question or participate. They are called "equity sticks" because they allow you to call on students equitably. Wheel of Names is a good way to call on students because it takes the responsibility off of you. Students know that you didn't pick them; the wheel did!

Create one wheel for each class, with the names of the students in that class. When it's time to ask a question, share the tab with the wheel, and spin it.

You can save each wheel by clicking the Save option in the ribbon at the top of the web page. Then you can jump between wheels by clicking Open.


Four more tips below
2. Select problems or examples

Let's say your students are practicing multiplying whole numbers. Create a wheel of numbers 1-10, or another number range, or even fractions, based on the students' level.

Then you could either spin the wheel in front of the class, or click Share at the top of the web page. If you click Share, you will get a link to the wheel you just created, that you can share with your students in Google Classroom, Google Meet, Zoom, etc.

This makes it more exciting for students than getting a worksheet or slides with problems to practice. Also, you can add pictures of models or base-ten blocks to make it more visual and engaging.

3. Select a book chapter or character

Add the chapters or character names from a book to the wheel. Share the link with students and let them spin the wheel. If it's a wheel of characters, they will have to do an analysis of that character. If it's a wheel of chapters, perhaps they should write a summary or analysis of that chapter.

4. Select a writing prompt

Enter a list of writing prompts, share the wheel with students, let them spin it, and then write something based on the prompt that wins.

You could also spin the wheel for the class and let all students write based on the one prompt that wins.

5. Link to a Google Spreadsheet

Click More in the ribbon at the top of the page and then click Import Google Spreadsheet. After you sign in with Google and allow the website to view your spreadsheets, you can select a spreadsheet and which column in that sheet should be imported to the wheel.

The Google Spreadsheet could be from a Google Form. If you send out a form to parents or students and they fill it out, you can then spin and select one or more random entries that they made.
